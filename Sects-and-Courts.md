# Sects and courts

Out in the world, Kindred live hidden inside ordinary settlements and answer to sects. Your
colony has a sect (set by its first vampire) and a standing with each one, shown in the Kindred
main tab.

## The sects

| Sect | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Camarilla** | The largest sect of Kindred, ruled by elders and princes. It keeps the Masquerade and the Traditions, and punishes those who break them. |
| **Sabbat** | The Sword of Caine. Packs of Kindred who reject their elders and their own lost humanity, bound to each other by shared blood and at war with the Camarilla. |
| **Anarchs** | Kindred who threw out their princes. They keep the Masquerade and little else, run free states under barons, and trade favours without anyone keeping score from on high. |
| **Independents** | Clans that answer to no sect: the Assamites, the Followers of Set, the Giovanni and the Ravnos. Each keeps its own haven and minds its own business. |

| Sect | Likes | Hates |
|---|---|---|
| Camarilla | Keeping the Masquerade, sanctioned Embraces, paid debts | Breaches, diablerie, unsanctioned childer, Thaumaturgy outside the Tremere |
| Sabbat | Strength, Paths, the Vaulderie, diablerie of elders | Camarilla ties, weakness, Humanity |
| Anarchs | Autonomy, helping each other | Being told what to do |
| Independents | Business | Depends on the clan |

You can change sides. Defecting angers the sect you leave, and it may hunt the whole coterie
for a season.

### The Camarilla's Traditions

| Tradition | In the game |
|---|---|
| Masquerade | The breach score. See [The Masquerade and hunters](Masquerade-and-Hunters.md) |
| Domain | Present yourself when you visit a court |
| Progeny | An Embrace needs permission |
| Accounting | A sire answers for its childe's mistakes |
| Hospitality | Harming a guest costs standing |
| Destruction | Killing another Kindred needs the Prince's say-so |

Breaking one only costs you if someone finds out.

## Courts

A court is a group of Kindred hiding in a settlement. The mortals there don't know. A world has a
few of them, and you only see one on the map once you've learned about it: from an envoy, a
captured vampire, a sleeper, a lore text, or gossip about a court near you. Your vampires also learn of a
court by meeting one of its members, or by a court you know vouching for it.

Courts change while you play, and the Chronicle in the Kindred overview reports it. A Prince falls
only to a plot inside the court (see [Coteries](#coteries)). A city can fall
to a Sabbat siege or an Anarch revolt, and the Camarilla can take it back. A siege always comes
from a court of another faction: courts in the same faction are allies and never fight each other.
A court only attacks one it can match, and a court no rival can reach may face its own Anarchs
instead. While the world has fewer courts than it started with, Kindred from elsewhere found a new
one about once a year. With Garou loaded, werewolf septs raid courts near them, and with Imbued,
hunter cells hunt them.

**Visiting.** Send a caravan and pick **Visit the court at X**. It walks into the town as
guests, and every vampire in it is presented on arrival. A caravan with no Kindred gets in too,
but only as visitors to an ordinary town. A court vampire who sees a Garou of yours change shape
or use a Gift there turns the whole city on you. While your
Kindred are there, in the town or in a caravan on its tile, the court's window (from the Courts
page of the Kindred tab) opens in person: Elysium, rumours, gifts, teaching and hand-overs.

**A hostile town.** A town at war with you takes no guests, but a caravan with a Kindred can pick
**Slip into the court at X**. It meets the court on the town's tile without walking in. The
town's guards catch it 40% of the time, 7% less for each dot of Obfuscate the best-hidden vampire
in the caravan has (never below 5%). A caught caravan has to fight its way into the town.

**Talking.** In a court's town you can talk to anyone of the court's faction. See
[Talking to people](#talking-to-people).

**Attacking.** A court's city is streets and houses around the court's hall in the middle,
built in its sect's style: Elysium's gallery and cells, the Sabbat's chapel and fight pit, the
Anarchs' rant hall and garage. Every vampire of the court has a coffin by rank. Its Kindred defend it, with a guard of three to six ghoul retainers in old-world gear.

### When a court falls

A court's Kindred are real people, and they outlive its fall. Some die, some go missing, and the rest
move to a court of their sect, their own faction first, or found a new one when three or more have
nowhere to go. The same happens when the court's town is razed, by you or by another mod.

- **The Masquerade.** Each court has a rating, shown in words on its inspect pane once you know the
  court: Secure, Frayed, Cracking, Breaking. Crowds of Kindred, feuds and your own breaches nearby
  wear it down; time and a Sheriff and Keeper in their seats mend it. A court with a worse Masquerade
  is attacked more often.
- **Sieges.** When a war is about to break a court you know, it is under siege for a few days first,
  and a quest lets you go and help. Who dies on the map is dead in the world.
- **Letters.** A court you know falling gets a letter: who did it, if anyone knows, a few of the dead
  by name, who is missing. Taking a court's town yourself says so. Rumors fill in the rest later, and
  the missing turn up again weeks or seasons on.
- **Refugees.** Survivors who like the colony and are within reach may ask to join, as an offer in
  the Quests tab open for two days.
- **Your rank** moves to the court most of the survivors went to, one step lower.
- **An empty domain.** When a court near you falls and you have a Kindred in good standing with its
  sect, an offer in the Quests tab gives you the city, with your eldest Kindred of that sect as
  Prince. It stays open for ten days.
- **Ties.** Your vampires take a mood hit when their sire's or childer's court falls.

Core's settings, under The world of darkness, have a switch for all of this (off: a fallen home's people just vanish), a bloodiness slider for falls with no battle, and a world pace slider.

### Rank

Rank belongs to one vampire at one court.

| Rank | How | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| Unpresented | | Nothing. Feeding there is poaching |
| Acknowledged | Present yourself | Missions, feeding rights, hospitality |
| Established | Several missions, no open scandal | Teachers, bigger boons |
| Officer | A seat and a mission for the Prince | The office's bonus, a say in blood hunts |
| Primogen | Eldest of your clan there, high standing | The right to Embrace |
| Prince | Recognition, or deposing the one there | Rule over that court |

Each step past Established names a rival who will scheme against you.

### Missions

Taken from the court window, never forced on you. They use vanilla quests: silence a witness,
carry a sealed item, deliver a vessel, hunt a diablerist, guard Elysium, recover a relic, clear a
rogue lair, bring a childe home, find a Golconda mentor, destroy a wight. They pay in boons, rank,
standing and teachers, rarely silver, and experience for every colony vampire who saw it through.
Assamites get contracts no one else can take.

Three missions are **investigations**: silence a witness, find who is feeding in the domain, bring
a runaway childe home. The quest names two to four settlements nearby. A caravan with a colony
vampire picks **Investigate** at one, once a night: Perception + Investigation against 7, two
dice more with Auspex 2. Two leads reveal where the quarry is. A botch warns it, and the deadline
comes three days sooner. A runaway childe can be captured, or right-clicked and talked round with
Charisma + Social, then handed over at the court.

The nearest court you know and stand well with also sends requests through the storyteller, like
any vanilla quest:

- **Host a conclave.** Three or four of its Kindred meet at your colony from after dark until an
  hour before dawn. The Sabbat or hunters come for them halfway through. Keep them all alive.
- **Shelter a refugee.** One vampire for one night, with its enemies close behind.
- **Lend a ghoul.** The court names one of your ghouls and borrows it for five days.
- **Bring goods.** Blood vials, blood wine, gold or medicine, by caravan to the court's town.

Each pays silver and a few vials of blood, with 10 goodwill and 10 court standing. One let lapse
costs half that.

### Coteries

A Camarilla or Anarch court is split into coteries: the Prince's own, made of the Prince and the
officers, and up to three rivals, each led by its eldest and never fewer than two. A rival
left with one member takes in a plain member from a bigger coterie, or breaks up. A Sabbat pack or an Independent haven is
one coterie, and nobody there plots. A court from an older save gets its coteries the first time
it's checked. They show on the court window's **Coteries** page, which is hidden when a court has
only one.

**Temperament.** Each coterie takes its temperament from its leader, and it changes when a new
leader takes over. It decides what the coterie asks for and what it pays.

| Temperament | Wants | Pays, on top of the boon | Its missions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tyrant | Control of rivals' herds, ghouls and havens | Silver | Raid a rival's herd, seize a rival's ghoul, storm a rival's haven, collect tribute |
| Scholar | Texts, relics, rituals | A lore text | Steal a text from a Keeper, recover a relic from a ruin, escort a scholar, bring back a ritual |
| Trickster | The court shaken up | Any of the others | Ruin a rival's Elysium, spread a rumour, free a rival's prisoner, swap a sealed item |
| Traditionalist | The Traditions kept | Standing, sometimes leave to Embrace | Destroy an unsanctioned childe, bring a breaker to court, clean up a breach, judge a dispute |
| Schemer | Boons and secrets | A boon a size bigger | Plant forged evidence, steal a boon, gather a secret, broker a deal |
| Zealot | War on the enemy sect and hunters | A weapon | Raid an enemy sect's site, hunt a hunter cell, burn a ghoul farm, send fighters to a sect war |

Each coterie keeps up to two offers on top of the court's own, and the court still has at most
three at a time. The missions play out three ways:

- **A site** a few days out (herd, haven, ruin, prisoner, enemy site, ghoul farm): clear it.
- **A quarry** that comes through your lands within a few nights (ghoul, courier, childe, breaker,
  hunters): bring it down. The ghoul and the breaker have to end up as your prisoners.
- **An errand** at a court: take a caravan there with the vampire who took it and press
  **Do the errand** under Missions, Under way. It's one roll, shown in the mission's tooltip, and a
  failed roll fails the mission. Some need more: bringing back a ritual needs Thaumaturgy or
  Necromancy, gathering a secret Auspex or Obfuscate, judging a dispute a Primogen seat, ruining an
  Elysium the gathering itself, and sending fighters three colonists in the caravan who can fight
  (some come back hurt).

**Power.** Each coterie's power runs from 0 to 20, drawn as ten cells. The Prince's starts at 10 to
14, a rival at 4 to 10. A mission done for a coterie adds 1, or 2 for a major one. A mission aimed at
a coterie takes 1 away, and so does each member it loses. Every few days one coterie may gain or lose
a point on its own. A vacant seat is likeliest to go to a member of the strongest coterie. Do five
missions for a coterie and it clears one scandal against your Kindred there each season.

**Working against the Prince.** An offer that hurts another coterie says which one under its name,
in orange when it's the Prince's own. A mission aimed at the Prince's coterie rolls to see if you were
found out: Manipulation + Social against 6, a die easier with Obfuscate. If you're caught, the court
thinks 5 to 10 less of you and the vampire gets a scandal.

**Plots.** When a rival stays within 3 power of the Prince's coterie for 30 days, it starts to plot.
A Kindred of yours with rank there gets a letter; otherwise the news reaches the Chronicle later.
The showdown comes 20 to 40 days on. Until then each side offers one **deciding mission**, which
counts 2 power for its side at the showdown and puts you on that side. You can also pick a side on
the Coteries page: **Back the Prince**, **Back the plotters** or **Stay out**. At the showdown each
side's chance is its share of the two scores. The losers' leader is destroyed or flees, and each of
its members is destroyed, flees, or goes over to the winners. After a showdown, win or lose, that
court has no new plot for a year, so it changes Prince once a year at most.

| Your side | What happens |
|---|---|
| Backed the winners | The new Prince honours the old one's boons to you and raises the biggest a size. Your ranked Kindred there go up a step, to Primogen at most. A drop pod brings the spoils of the losers' havens, by their temperament: lore from Scholars, silver and a weapon from Tyrants and Zealots, a boon from Schemers, blood vials from Traditionalists, any of it from Tricksters. One time in three a loser who fled comes too, staked and in torpor, as your prisoner |
| Backed the losers | The old Prince's boons stay if the Prince fled and are lost if the Prince was destroyed. The winners answer by temperament. A Tyrant names your busiest vampire and sends enforcers after them. A Scholar shuts its teachers, rituals and lore texts to you for a year. A Trickster forgives you for a minor boon. A Traditionalist puts your Kindred back to acknowledged. A Schemer wants a minor boon and gives each of your Kindred there a scandal. A Zealot costs you 10 to 20 standing |
| Stayed out | The old Prince's boons shrink a size (a trivial one stays trivial), and are lost if the old Prince was destroyed |

The plot section on the Coteries page shows both scores and the plotters' chance. Hover it for the
rule.

### Your place in a coterie

Each coterie has a regard for the colony, from -10 to 10, drawn as a thin bar under its power:
warm at 4 or more, hostile at -4 or less, cool in between. It drifts a step back toward 0 each
season. A hostile coterie offers you no work.

| Regard | Change |
|---|---|
| A mission done for it | +1 |
| Backing it in a plot it wins | +2 |
| A mission done against it | -2 |
| Backing the other side in its plot | -2 |
| Each of its members the court knows you harmed | -3 |
| Each season one of your vampires sits in a coterie it plots against | -1 |
| Leaving it | -2, and 1 of its power |

The detail pane lists each step with a tick or a cross for each thing it needs, and the button
under it.

- **Join.** A vampire Established at the court or higher, five missions done for the coterie by
  anyone in the colony, and its regard at 0 or more. Each vampire is in one coterie per court at
  most. While one of yours is in it, a mission done for it adds 1 more power, and each of your
  members there counts the power it gave. A rival can still offer work aimed at your coterie: the
  offer says so in orange, and taking it gives your vampire there a scandal.
- **Take the lead.** Once the power your missions gave it since joining is half its power or more.
  With two of yours in it, the one who gave more (then the one who joined first) leads. You pick its
  temperament once, for good. A coterie you lead offers you no work, and it keeps you as leader
  until your vampire dies, leaves the court's ranks or leaves the coterie.
- **Move against the Prince.** As leader, within 3 power of the Prince's coterie, after the year's
  wait, at a court of your sect (an Anarch colony can take a Camarilla court, which becomes Anarch).
  The plot starts at once and the showdown comes 20 to 40 days later.

In a plot your vampires stand with their own coteries, and the Back buttons are hidden. One on the
losing side leaves its coterie. Win as plotters and your leader becomes Prince: your other Kindred
in the coterie take the seats the losers held, every member there owes the new Prince a trivial
boon, and the court page gets a Your domain section.

- **Tribute:** 100 silver and 25 for each member, by drop pod each season.
- **The Prince's word:** your own vampires get leave to Embrace, a pardon and a blood hunt lifted
  there for nothing.
- **Call a blood hunt** on any member. It is just when the member's coterie is hostile to you or it
  is on your grudge list. Otherwise your sect thinks 5 less of you.

Rivals plot against your coterie as against any Prince. Lose a showdown and your vampires there go
back to acknowledged, leave their coteries, and the court shuts its doors to you for a year: no
missions, services or joining.

### Taken and slain unseen

A court only knows the colony took or destroyed one of its vampires if someone who knew the victim
saw it and lived to tell. That means the court's own people, its other Kindred, the victim's kin and
friends, and its ghouls. Strangers, traders and other factions only saw a fight. A witness has to be
awake and within 20 cells with a clear line of sight. They show on the witness alert, and the court
hears once one of them leaves the map: kill them, take them too, or keep them as prisoners and the
secret holds. A prisoner who is let go or escapes tells their court who held them. So does a captive
you release.

| The court learns you | Cost |
|---|---|
| Took one of its vampires | 5 court standing, 15 for the Prince |
| Destroyed one | 10 court standing; the Prince 30, and 30 with its sect too |

A vampire the colony holds, awake or in torpor, fades from its court whether the court knows or not.
The court only knows it's gone.

- At once its coterie loses 1 power, and 1 more every 10 days, 3 at most. The Chronicle says it
  vanished.
- After 15 days it loses its office, which passes on as if it had been destroyed.
- After a year (60 days) the court strikes it off. If it ever comes back, it rejoins as a plain
  member.

The court window marks your captives "(your prisoner)" in the members list.

## Talking to people

Select a colonist and right-click someone to talk to them: a prisoner (**Question ...**), a witness
(see [Witnesses](Masquerade-and-Hunters.md#silencing-a-witness)), or someone in a court's, sept's
or hunter cell's town (**Talk to ...**). Your colonist walks over and the talk window opens.
Pick what to ask about, then how to put it. Each way shows its roll under the button.

| Way | Roll |
|---|---|
| Kindly | Charisma + Social at 6 |
| Firmly | Manipulation + Social at 6 |
| Threaten | Strength + Social at 7. It hurts their mood |
| Bribe | 40 silver, always answers. One of the court's own costs double and its leader triple |
| Dominate | Manipulation + Social against their Willpower, from Dominate 2. Never on a vampire of lower generation |
| Presence | Charisma + Social against their Willpower, for a vampire with Presence but too little Dominate |
| Owe a boon | At a court, to one of its Kindred: the colony owes them a minor boon instead of paying silver |

Each roll is a point harder against anything supernatural. Nobody lies, so a poor roll just gets
nothing.

In a town, each person answers one question, then wants three days before the next (a setting).
You can ask:

- **What's the news?** Word of the world's courts, septs and cells, or the court's own gossip.
- **Where is...** Pick a place from what they could know of: another court you don't know yet
  (Kindred speaking to Kindred), or a torpor crypt (a court's own Kindred, and most of them
  don't know of one). The question only shows up when there's somewhere to ask about.
- **Have you seen...** A colonist someone took. The settlement holding them goes on the map.
- **What troubles you?** The court has work: a new offer on its missions.

A threat costs the town 3 goodwill whatever the roll. Using Dominate or Presence on one of the
court's own in its town breaks Elysium if the court notices, which it does four times in ten. Your
standing there falls by 10 and the court thinks less of the colony.

A vampire talking in a sept's or a cell's town passes for an ordinary visitor. Asking kindly and
bribing are safe. A threat gives it away one time in five, a Discipline about one in three, a failed
roll one in ten and a botch one in two. Found out, the town turns on the colony.

## Boons

Kindred trade favours. The boon ledger in the Kindred main tab shows who owes whom and how much:
trivial, minor, major or life. A bigger boon pays for a smaller price, but smaller boons never add
up to a bigger one.

| Size | Earned by | Buys at court |
|---|---|---|
| Trivial | An errand (a vessel, a sealed casket), six blood vials, a low ritual cast for the court, a hungry guest sent home fed, a Nosferatu secret, three corpses for the Giovanni | Word of another court, a night's feeding for one vampire |
| Minor | Standing guard at Elysium, scouting a rogue band, a day's shelter for a court's vampire, an occult item or lore text, a level 3 ritual, a Toreador masterwork, handing over a fugitive | Permission to Embrace |
| Major | Hunting a diablerist, a contract, destroying a wight, releasing a captured vampire, an artifact or clan relic, a level 4 or 5 ritual, teaching a Discipline you have at 3 dots | A teacher, a scandal buried, witnesses silenced, a step up in rank, the hunters' files lost, an elder destroyed, the court's fighters, an artifact |
| Life | Getting a court's vampire through a hunters' raid alive (sanctuary) | A blood hunt lifted, a pardon for diablerie |

Every boon in the ledger says why it is owed and at which court. Paying for a service, you pick
which boon to spend. Any vampire can spend a boon the colony earned. Credit is harder: a trivial or
minor service needs an Acknowledged vampire at that court, a major one Established, a life boon an
Officer, and no court gives credit while the colony owes a boon anywhere.

The Prince's artifact is picked from the collection that court actually keeps, so a court with
none can't sell one.

A court takes one favour a season (a gift, a ritual or teaching), through **Offer a favour** in the
court window.

A boon is owed by one vampire, not by its court. If it is destroyed the boon is gone; if it flees
or moves, the boon goes with it. The ledger is a table of debtor, seat, where it is now and size,
and a debtor nobody can find is greyed with a "?".

- **Court help**, on the Boons page of the Kindred tab, lists what each court you know can do
  for you now, by who would do it: the Prince for leave to Embrace, a pardon, a lifted hunt or an
  artifact; the Seneschal for a step up or feeding rights; the Sheriff or a Hound for the hunters or
  fighters; the Harpy for a scandal; the Keeper for a lore text; a sorcerer for a ritual it knows;
  an ancilla or elder with the dots for a Discipline (neonates don't teach). It has a tab for each
  price, trivial to life. A boon owed by the one who does it is called in; otherwise you can hand
  over a boon someone else owes you, as long as it is the price or bigger. No change comes back:
  a major boon spent on a minor service is gone.
- **Credit** is owed to the vampire who gave the service, and ends if that vampire is destroyed.
- **Call it in** from the Kindred tab once you know where the debtor is. You pick one thing up to
  the boon's size: blood, a vessel, a word at its court, an introduction to another court, finding
  someone you lost track of, shelter from a blood hunt, a ghoul for a season, an errand run for
  you, a vote in a coming showdown, the debtor and its ghouls in a fight (more ghouls the older it
  is, four with a seat), or the debtor for good. Each clan has a favour of its own on top: Brujah
  rabble, a Gangrel guide, a Malkavian prophecy, a Nosferatu secret, Toreador art, a Tremere
  ritual, Ventrue money, Lasombra shadows round a caravan, Tzimisce fleshcraft, an Assamite
  contract, a Setite vice, a Giovanni ghost's tale, a Ravnos decoy, Salubri healing, a song from a
  Daughter of Cacophony, a Kiasyd lore text, a Gargoyle guard.

New ways to earn one: carry a downed friendly vampire to safety (minor) or wake one from torpor
with colony blood (major), keep quiet about a visitor you saw break the Masquerade (or report it
to its Prince for 5 standing), or buy a trivial favour from a member with 5 blood vials or 200
silver from a caravan, once a season per court. A boon offered this way comes as a letter where
you pick the boon, standing at its court (3, 6, 10 or 15 by size), silver (50, 150, 400 or 1000),
or nothing.

When a court calls in what the colony owes, it asks for a task of the same size: a vessel, a
scouting run, a diablerist destroyed, or a far stronger lair for a life boon. A refused debt is a
scandal. A refused life boon brings a blood hunt.

### The anonymous benefactor

Sometimes gifts arrive with no name on them: blood vials, then something rare, then a hint. Weeks
later the sender is named and the bill comes due.

## Your colony as a domain

With high Camarilla standing, three Kindred, enough wealth and a friendly court, you can petition
for your colony to be recognised as a domain. An archon comes to inspect it over two nights. Then
you assign offices to your own Kindred:

| Office | Bonus |
|---|---|
| Prince | Sanctions your own Embraces and blood hunts |
| Seneschal, Sheriff, Keeper of Elysium, Harpy | A small bonus fitting the job |

### Elysium at home

Once a season a domain (or an Anarch or Sabbat colony) can host a night for a court's Kindred:
Elysium, a rant or an esbat. Guests keep to the party spot and leave before dawn. A quiet night
earns standing, more in a fine room and more again with your own Toreador art on show. Violence
against a guest, or an outsider seeing a vampire at work, ruins it.

## The Scourge

Camarilla courts seat a Scourge, who destroys vampires of the 14th generation and higher. If a colony vampire's blood is that thin, the nearest Camarilla court can send its Scourge with a small band, by night, and the band goes for that vampire first. Your standing with the court doesn't stop it.

A colony with its own domain can name a Scourge of its own in its offices. That stops the hunts and brings missions to cull thin-bloods for standing. A setting turns Scourges off.

## Blood hunt

A blood hunt is the Camarilla's death sentence, called for diablerie, a refused life boon, or a
shattered Masquerade. The hunted vampire can't visit courts, archons raid far more often, and
anyone may destroy it without penalty. Paying a life boon at a court lifts it.

### The blood hounds

While the colony shelters a vampire under blood hunt, the nearest friendly Camarilla court sends
its hounds by night, at most every six days. The letter gives three answers:

- **Hand the vampire over.** The court owes you a minor boon, the hunt is lifted, and the vampire
  is gone.
- **Let them search.** Three of the court's Kindred walk round the home area for six hours. One
  who sees the hunted within 20 cells finds it: -10 standing with the court and the Camarilla, and
  the hounds attack. Obfuscate, a coffin or being away from the map keeps it out of sight.
- **Turn them away.** -4 standing with both.

## The Red List

The Camarilla's list of the Kindred it most wants destroyed. Five Anathema roam the world map,
whether the colony has Kindred or not, each a 6th-generation vampire with every Discipline it has
at its limit, harder to hurt and quicker than any elder, guilty of some great crime. A name struck
off is replaced a quadrum after the last one went on. Only a colony in the Camarilla knows the list:
anyone else gets no letters, sees no marks and hears no rumours, and meets an Anathema as a stranger.

- **The mark.** Each Anathema is a mark on the world map, 10 to 40 tiles from home, moving a few
  tiles every three days or so, and about one move in four it stops in a town. It shows only while
  it's within 25 tiles of a home or a colony caravan, or on a map.
- **The Red List window**, from the Overview tab of the Kindred window or from the mark's button,
  shows only what the colony knows. The name is always known. Clan, generation, crime, retinue and
  each Discipline are rolled when the name goes on the list, and every few days a rumour adds one
  more thing or shows the mark for two days. Facing it tells its clan and retinue, seeing it use a
  power tells that Discipline's level, and knowing the clan names its clan Disciplines.
- **The hunt.** A caravan sent to the mark always finds it, and fights it with up to 3 ghouls on a
  map of its own. By night it fights at once. By day it's already sheltered, in a haven one of its
  powers makes or dug into the earth, with its ghouls on guard. Opened before dusk, it's in the sun.
- **In a town.** A caravan that walks into the town by night finds it among the townsfolk with its
  retinue, keeping to one spot. By day it's out of sight. It passes for a stranger as on a visit.
- **A visit.** One within 12 tiles may cross the colony's land by night with its retinue. If it has
  never fought the colony, it doesn't know who lives there and walks on through. Hurt it or anyone
  with it, or use a power on any of them, and they all attack. One that has fought the colony before
  comes to stalk, waiting in the dark for someone alone.
- **The bounty**, when the colony had a hand in its Final Death: +15 standing with the Camarilla, a
  major boon and +10 standing from the nearest Camarilla court, 3 experience for the vampire who did
  it, and a line in the chronicle. One that gets away stays on the list and comes back healed.

The setting "The Red List" on the World page turns it off. Turned back on, a fresh list comes in
one letter.

## The Sabbat

A Sabbat colony has no Prince. Instead:

- **Offices.** The Ductus leads the pack, and the Pack Priest makes the Vaulderie stronger.
- **The Vaulderie.** A button in the main tab, once a week at night, for two or more Kindred.
  Each pays a point of blood. Every pair gains **Vinculum**, a loyalty to each other, and every
  blood bond they held to a vampire breaks.
- **The creed.** Each season the Sabbat thinks less of you for every vampire still on Humanity,
  and more for every one on a Path.

## The Anarchs

In a Camarilla domain, a colony vampire who dislikes the Prince (or a Brujah, Gangrel or
Caitiff) may stir up trouble. You can give them a say or put it down, and putting it down can
send them off to join the Anarchs.

## The Independents

Each Independent clan keeps a haven you can visit like a court, with one piece of business:

| Clan | Business |
|---|---|
| Giovanni | Three corpses buy a trivial boon |
| Assamite | Five blood vials pay for one of your enemies to disappear |
| Followers of Set | A drug buys a string on a mortal in a court's city |
| Ravnos | A trivial boon buys a stolen item. Sometimes the owner finds out |

## Influence over mortals

A **string** is a mortal in another settlement who answers to you. Ghoul, bond or Dominate an
outsider and let them go home, or work one from a caravan. Strings can be pulled for favours, and
found out. Ventrue hold more. Followers of Set can make them with drugs.
